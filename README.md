Choosing salon booking software is not just about taking appointments online. The right tool should help you keep clients coming back, get paid on time, and spend less of your day on admin. This guide explains what to look for, what to ask before you commit, and how a lifetime-licence option compares with a monthly subscription.

## 1. Start with how you actually work

Before comparing features, answer these questions:

- Do you work alone, or with a team sharing chairs, rooms, or treatment beds?
- Do clients book mostly by phone, Instagram, or walk-in — and would an online booking page save you messages back and forth?
- Do you need client notes (allergies, formulas, preferences, past treatments) at your fingertips?
- How do you invoice and track who has paid, who still owes, and what you earned this week?
- Do you send reminders to reduce no-shows?

Software that fits your real workflow will get used every day. Software that only looks good in a demo often ends up abandoned.

## 2. The essentials: booking

A good booking system should let you:

- Set your services, durations, and prices clearly, so clients know what they are booking.
- Show real availability, so double bookings and awkward gaps are avoided.
- Let clients book themselves through a shareable booking page, without needing to call during working hours.
- Reschedule or cancel easily, for you and for the client.
- See your day, week, and team at a glance in a booking calendar.

Ask: can a new client find a slot, book it, and get a confirmation without you having to reply manually?

## 3. Client records (CRM): remember every client

Client records are the memory of your business. Look for:

- A simple client profile with contact details and booking history.
- Notes on preferences, treatments, products used, and anything to check next time.
- A way to find a returning client quickly, instead of searching through messages or paper cards.

Good records make repeat visits smoother, more personal, and safer — especially for colour, lash, nail, and skin services where history matters.

## 4. Invoices and finances: get paid without the paperwork

Booking software should make money management easier, not harder. Useful features include:

- Branded invoices you can send after an appointment.
- A clear record of payments, outstanding balances, and income over time.
- Less time copying totals between notebooks, spreadsheets, and payment apps.

Ask: after a busy Saturday, can you tell in a few minutes who paid, who still owes, and what you earned?

## 5. Reminders and follow-up: fewer no-shows, more rebookings

No-shows cost time and income. Reminders help clients remember, and a quick follow-up can turn one visit into a regular booking.

Look for:

- Appointment reminders before the visit.
- An easy way to rebook at checkout, before the client leaves.
- Intake forms, where relevant, so you collect the information you need before the appointment starts.

## 6. Lifetime licence vs. monthly subscription: the cost question

Most salon software is sold as a monthly subscription. You pay every month for as long as you use it, and the cost keeps adding up year after year.

A lifetime licence works differently: you pay once for the licence and keep using the software, without an ongoing monthly fee for that licence. That can be especially attractive for solo stylists and small salons watching every expense.

When comparing the two, check:

- What is included in the price, and what, if anything, costs extra?
- Can you still access your client and booking data if you stop paying a subscription?
- How long would it take for monthly fees to exceed the one-time licence cost?
- Does the tool cover booking, clients, and invoices in one place, or would you need several subscriptions?

There is no single right answer. The right choice is the one whose total cost and features fit your business for years, not just for a free trial.

## 7. Checklist for solo stylists, lash, nail, and spa owners

Use this checklist before you choose:

- [ ] Online booking with a shareable booking page clients can use any time.
- [ ] Booking calendar that shows your real availability.
- [ ] Client records (CRM) with history and notes.
- [ ] Branded invoices and a simple view of your finances.
- [ ] Reminders to reduce no-shows.
- [ ] Intake forms for new clients, where needed.
- [ ] Works on your phone as well as on a computer (for example, as a PWA you can use like an app).
- [ ] Clear pricing — subscription or lifetime licence — with no surprise essentials.
- [ ] A way to export or keep your client data.
- [ ] Support and guidance when you are setting up.

If a tool ticks most of these boxes and fits how you work, try it with real bookings for a week before deciding.

## 9. A simple way to decide

1. List your must-haves from the checklist above.
2. Shortlist two or three tools that cover booking, clients, and invoices together.
3. Compare total cost over two years, not just the first month.
4. Test with real clients, real services, and real invoices.
5. Choose the tool you will actually open every morning.

The best salon booking software is the one that saves you time, helps clients rebook, and gets you paid — without adding another monthly bill you dread.
